Sida Ye

About

I am a Postdoctoral Scientist at Harvard Medical School, Brigham and Women’s Hospital, and the Howard Hughes Medical Institute in the laboratory of Prof. Matthew K. Waldor.

I am a computational biologist with training in mathematical modeling, machine learning, microbiology, parasitology, and experimental biology. I develop computational and genome-scale experimental approaches to understand pathogen fitness, gene essentiality, colonization, and drug susceptibility.

My current work integrates transposon insertion mutagenesis, statistical modeling, long-read sequencing, epigenomics, trajectory analysis, biological networks, and interactive visualization.

Research Interests

  • Pathogen functional genomics: genome-scale transposon screens and gene-essentiality analysis.
  • Host–pathogen interactions: spatiotemporal analysis of bacterial colonization across host tissues.
  • Apicomplexan parasite biology: functional genomics of Plasmodium and Babesia.
  • Computational method development: statistical learning, machine learning, network biology, and multidimensional data analysis.
  • Multi-omics and long-read genomics: transcriptomics, epigenomics, structural variation, noncoding RNA, and transposable elements.

Current Research Projects

Spatiotemporal MultiCAST screens of Vibrio cholerae colonization

I lead the computational analysis of genome-scale in vivo MultiCAST screens across gastrointestinal sites and infection time points. The analysis integrates MAGeCK MLE, dynamic time warping, cofitness analysis, graphical Gaussian models, tensor methods, and interactive visualization.

The essential genome of Plasmodium knowlesi

I led the computational analysis, mathematical modeling, perturbation analysis, and web application development for this genome-scale functional study. I developed multiple insertion-based essentiality models and drug-response pipelines and contributed most of the multidimensional data visualizations.

Transposon mutagenesis in Babesia divergens

I constructed transposon mutant libraries, performed cold-shock and drug perturbation screens, and developed computational models for genome-wide gene-essentiality analysis and integration with single-cell RNA sequencing.

Inducible transposon mutagenesis in Plasmodium falciparum

In collaboration with the Prigge laboratory at Johns Hopkins University, I contributed to the design of an inducible transposon-mutagenesis platform and lead its computational analysis.

Epigenetic regulation through lactylation in Plasmodium falciparum

I developed automated CUT&RUN and ChIP-seq pipelines and statistical models to investigate lactylation-associated gene regulation and parasite sexual commitment.
