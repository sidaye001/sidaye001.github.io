Sida Ye Research Publications Resources Presentations Education CV Sida Ye Research Interests I am a Postdoctoral Scientist at Harvard Medical School, Brigham and Women’s Hospital, and HHMI in the laboratory of Prof. Matthew K. Waldor. I develop computational and genome-scale experimental approaches to understand pathogen fitness, gene essentiality, colonization, and drug susceptibility. My work bridges computational biology, microbiology, parasitology, and functional genomics. My research focuses on developing: • Genome-scale functional-genomics methods based on transposon insertion mutagenesis. • Statistical and machine-learning models for gene essentiality, fitness, and perturbation-response analysis. • Spatiotemporal models of pathogen colonization across host tissues and gastrointestinal environments. • Computational pipelines and interactive resources for long-read sequencing, epigenomics, and multidimensional functional-genomics data. Current Research Pathogen Functional Genomics My work uses transposon mutagenesis to identify essential genes, conditionally important pathways, and determinants of drug response in Plasmodium knowlesi, Plasmodium falciparum, and Babesia divergens. Spatiotemporal Host–Microbe Interactions In the Waldor laboratory, I lead computational analysis of spatiotemporal MultiCAST screens that profile Vibrio cholerae fitness across gastrointestinal sites and infection time points. The analysis integrates dynamic time warping, cofitness analysis, graphical Gaussian models, and multidimensional representation learning. Epigenomics and Long-Read Analysis I develop reproducible pipelines for CUT&RUN, ChIP-seq, long-read transcriptomics, structural-variation analysis, transposable-element analysis, and noncoding-RNA discovery in microbial and parasitic systems.
